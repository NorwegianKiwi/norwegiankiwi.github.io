# Revised illustration briefs

These supersede the density-first directions in the original `../PROMPTS.md`.
That file remains the provenance record for the currently installed images.
Tourist's exact executed prompts are in `tourist-v2-prompt.txt` and
`tourist-v2-correction.txt`. The following five scene briefs are prepared for
generation after the Tourist calibration checkpoint, not yet executed prompts.

## Shared generation instructions

Use case: illustration-story. Produce a 1536 × 1024 landscape, full-bleed
storybook painting with warm gouache texture and fine ink detail. Preserve the
originals' expressive, cute character designs and teal/coral/mustard palette.
Use the original as a style reference only; rebuild flawed spatial arrangements.

Establish the viewpoint, major surfaces and connected structures before adding
small stories. Each recurring character appears once: yellow raincoat/red
backpack child, blue cap/round spectacles child, small russet fox with rolled
map. Give figures coherent limbs and joints; make hands, grips, object handles,
foot placement, supports, occlusion and scale readable. Clothed animals are
intentional fantasy. Other fantasy is limited to the scene-specific rules below.
Avoid merged bodies, accidental duplicates, disconnected machinery, floating
props, pseudo-writing and arbitrary untraceable detail. If a construction is
too complex to draw clearly, simplify it. No visible grid, seams, border or text.

Distribute recognizable props, animals and environmental details across the
existing piece regions. Some calmer space is welcome. Interesting fragments do
not each need a complex action or an entire character. Coherence takes priority
over maximum density. Recheck the full composition after any local correction.

## Explorer — 40 pieces, eight columns × five rows

An elevated three-quarter view of one island hillside with a clearly delineated
cutaway along its lower rock face. The two children and fox explore together on
one visible path. A treehouse and one rope bridge have explicit trunks, anchor
points and supported landings. A single stream flows downhill into a pool;
dry caves are above its water level, and any underwater area has a clear boundary.
Keep cave passages physically separated by visible rock. Use birds, a monkey
with a stolen hat, roots, crystals, camping supplies and small carved objects
for discoveries. Avoid repeated protagonists and an unexplained submarine in a
dry cave. The cutaway is an illustration convention, not broken terrain.

## Navigator — 52 pieces, six rows of 9, 8, 9, 9, 8, 9

One coastal harbour viewed obliquely, with a clear waterline and an illustrative
underwater cross-section below it. The children and fox stand once on a timber
pier with continuous deck, railings and posts. Two modest boats float at that
waterline, with continuous hulls and simple traceable masts/rigging. A lighthouse
rests on a rocky foundation. One sealed submersible below the surface has one
distinct operator; humans or foxes do not breathe exposed underwater. Marine
animals have coherent natural bodies. Use coral, a crab picnic, small fish and
a clearly resting wreck for detail. Animal play is the declared fantasy; water,
buoyancy, supports, air spaces and vehicle construction remain coherent.

## Globetrotter — 56 pieces, eight columns × seven rows

An elevated view over a terraced town with consistent perspective and buildings
resting on visible streets or terraces. Limit major transport systems to one
tram route crossing a supported bridge, one cable car suspended from a continuous
line between two stations, and two balloons with clearly connected baskets and
ropes. Place the three main travellers together in the lower market square.
Use market stalls, a duck crossing, luggage, rooftop gardens, windows and small
animals for the remaining discoveries. Draw bicycles simply, with two wheels,
a coherent frame and sensible rider contact. The fantastical town and animal
travellers are intentional; disconnected tracks or floating trams are not.

## Cartographer — 44 pieces, six rows of 7, 8, 7, 7, 8, 7

An oblique view of a solid rectangular workshop table with a single consistent
perspective. The two children and fox stand behind it at one scale; the living
miniature landscape has a separate, consistent smaller scale. Paper map edges
remain traceable beneath mountains and towns. One miniature railway follows a
continuous track, crossing a river on a visibly supported bridge. A magical
river spills from a map edge into a teacup placed below it; the source and fall
are clear. Ordinary tools, rolled maps, pencils, jars and animal helpers supply
detail. Use simple recognizable instruments rather than a hybrid clock/map
machine. Only the living map and animal helpers are magical; object support,
liquid direction, hands and perspective remain coherent.

## World Master — 24 pieces, six columns × four rows

A small floating fictional planet viewed in three-quarter perspective. The
central celebration terrace supplies one clear standing plane for the three
main travellers, each appearing once. Surrounding miniature locations recall
earlier chapters through landmarks and props rather than duplicate characters.
Structures contact the planet's surface; their orientation follows the local
surface consistently. A short railway follows a supported terrace with every
vehicle aligned to its track. Balloons have ordinary connected baskets. Use a
picnic, birds, a suitcase cat, a submarine and flowers for distributed discoveries.
The floating planet and animal reunion are the intentional fantasy. Boats remain
in water, feet remain on ground, and suitcases do not become unexplained aircraft.
